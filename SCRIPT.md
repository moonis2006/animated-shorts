# "The Captain Who Fired Cheese" — original script v1 (2026-11-02)
Format: Tactical Fiction short (~59 s, ~3.7 words/s), original characters + story.
Facts: Battle off Montevideo, May 1841 (Guerra Grande); Commodore John Halstead Coe commanding the Uruguayan flagship against Admiral William Brown's Argentine squadron; ran out of 18-pounder iron shot while powder remained; loaded rock-hard stale Dutch Edam cheese wheels into the 18-pounder cannons; shots tore through enemy sails/rigging and shattered on the mainmast into yellow shrapnel; Brown's fleet mistook it for a secret new munition and retreated.

A navy captain once won a warship battle by firing stale cheese out of his cannons.
It's 1841 off the coast of Uruguay, and Commodore John Coe is locked in a gun duel with a bigger enemy flagship.
Ten minutes in, the gunner runs up with terrible news: the powder barrels are full, but they're completely out of iron cannonballs.
The enemy ship is turning in to finish them off, and the crew is ready to wave the white flag.
Then the captain remembers the kitchen below deck is stacked with round wheels of Dutch cheese that went stale months ago.
He orders the crew to roll the cheeses up the stairs and ram them straight down the eighteen-pounder cannons.
The whole broadside fires at once, and a dozen yellow wheels scream across the water and smash right through the enemy mast.
Luckily for the enemy crew, they were only getting hit with dairy.
Unluckily for the enemy crew, that dairy was moving at nine hundred miles an hour.
You see, when round Dutch cheese dries out at sea, the moisture leaves and the proteins lock together like solid oak.
Ever left a block of cheese uncovered in the back of the fridge? You can barely dent it with a knife—and fired from a cannon, it shatters on impact like shrapnel.
The enemy admiral thought they were using a secret new exploding weapon and ordered his entire fleet to retreat.
So the next time someone says cheese isn't tactical, remind them it's undefeated at sea.
